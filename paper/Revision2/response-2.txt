	- we thank the reviewer for pushing us to be more precise in our claims and now include two key citations in our discussion on page YY. The seurat approach uses three possible normalizations log(scaled proportion), centred log-ratio and raw counts +1. The tool has several options for differential abundance testing, among them being the Wilcoxon test (default) and the DESeq2 tool. We now include citations to literature that advocated using pseudo-bulk approaches to analyzing single-cell data (Zimmerman 2021). Finally, we now cite "Confronting false discoveries in single-cell differential expression", by Squair et al which shows that single-cell data suffers from similar false-discoveries in randomized data as does bulk-RNA seq (Li et al) and microbiome data (Skinnider). In particular Figure 4d shows that many of these FD are small-difference:low variance genes or high difference:high variance genes,  precisely what is removed by adding a small amount of uncertainty to the normalization. This paper also shows that the entire gamut of tools for DA analysis (including those used in our modelling and included in th seurat package) suffer from the high FDR problem. Additionally, a more recent benchmarking paper (Junttila, https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/23/5/bbac286/6649780) shows that these tools are in common use in the field. Indeed, together the two bencmarking papers indicate that bulk RNA-seq DA tools are common options. We believe that this shows that the approaches we are modelling in our submission are widely used in the ssRNA sequencing field.
	
	- the claim of "universal scale applicability" is intended to mean, "Given a high throughput sequencing dataset that we want to perform DA/DE analysis on, a more appropriate way to do so that controls for the FDR is to incorporate uncertainty in the normalization. Our results show that adding scale uncertainty provides a buffer for the FDR regardless of the data type, and does so in such a way that both likely types of FDR parts are excluded. Thus, this approach reduces the circular reasoning of the dual-cutoff approach which is common in these types of analyses."
